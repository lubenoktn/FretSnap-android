FretSnap – guide

FretSnap records riffs from a ToneX Plug (and other USB audio interfaces) to your phone, helps you practice (tuner, metronome, drums, looper) and keeps your recordings organized.

1. Connecting ToneX

  1. Plug the ToneX Plug into your phone (USB-C, or through an OTG adapter).
  2. Android may ask whether FretSnap may use the device – confirm. FretSnap may also open by itself.
  3. After a moment the label at the top shows ToneX Plug in green. While it shows an orange “Starting…”, ToneX is still waking up.
  4. Play – the waveform and the level bar must move.
FretSnap reads ToneX directly over USB, because the phone's audio system doesn't let it through. That's why Android doesn't “see” ToneX while you use FretSnap, and FretSnap sends sound to its headphones itself (metronome, drums, looper).

1a. Acoustic guitar (microphone)

  1. Without ToneX, FretSnap records with the built-in microphone – the top label shows Built-in microphone.
  2. In ⚙ Settings turn on 🎸 Acoustic guitar via microphone. The phone then stops processing the sound as it would for a phone call (noise suppression and automatic volume distort a guitar), and rumble below the lowest guitar note is filtered out (tapping on the body, vibrations).
  3. Place the phone about 30–50 cm (1–1.5 ft) from the guitar, with the microphone (bottom edge) pointing to where the neck meets the body. If the level bar stays in the red while you play, move the phone away or lower the gain.
The microphone also records the metronome, drums and backing coming from the speaker. Use wired headphones when recording with them (Bluetooth headphones lag). FretSnap reminds you when you start recording.

2. Main screen

Label at the top
Where the sound comes from: green = working, orange = starting, red = problem. Tap it to open the input settings.
Waveform “← 30 s”
What the input heard in the last 30 seconds – exactly what the ⟲ button saves. Newest is on the right, recorded parts are red.
Level bar
Green is fine, yellow is loud, red and a red edge = clipping. Channel levels are shown below it.
Gain ▾
Tap to show the slider; if the sound clips, turn it down.
Buttons
⟲ 30 s (save what just played), big ● (record), ♪ Tuner.
Tools
♩ metronome, 🥁 drums, Count-in / Lead-in; below them 🔁 Looper, 🎼 Scale, 🎹 Backing. When a tool is open, it takes the place of the riff list (the list comes back when you close it).
Played today
How long you played today and how many days in a row. Tap it to open a two-week diary.

3. Recording

Normal recording

  • Tap the big ●. The button pulses, the screen edge glows red and the time runs in the middle.
  • Tap again (■) to stop recording; the riff is saved.
  • Recording continues with the screen off (there's a notification). The screen doesn't turn off by itself while recording unless you change that in the settings.
  • When saving, silence at the start and end is trimmed and the loudness is evened out so riffs are equally loud (both can be turned off).

Count-in and lead-in

The label below the record button switches with each tap: Count-in (off) → Count-in ✓ → Lead-in 4 ✓.

  • Count-in: after tapping ●, one bar plays (metronome, or sticks if the drums are playing) and only then recording starts.
  • Lead-in: first the drums play three bars (and the backing, if it's on) so you can get into the groove. In the fourth bar the drums stop and the sticks count 4 – 3 – 2 – 1 while the numbers appear on screen. Recording starts on the first beat of the fifth bar and the drums keep playing. With the metronome it's the same, only the metronome clicks more quietly during the lead-in.

Tap the button during the count to cancel it. The tempo is saved with the riff.

⟲ 30 s – save after the fact

Played something good while not recording? Tap ⟲ – the last 30 seconds are saved.

📍 Markers while recording

While recording there's a 📍 Marker button below. Tap it at a good spot – the marker is saved with the riff and you can jump to it in the player.

Automatic recording (optional)

In the settings turn on “Record automatically when I start playing”. Recording starts by itself when you play (it also keeps a second before that so the first note isn't lost) and stops after 4 seconds of silence. Recording you start by hand doesn't stop by itself.

If FretSnap crashes or the phone dies while recording, the recording is recovered on the next start (the list shows “· recovered”).

4. Tuner

  • Tap ♪ Tuner, choose a tuning (Standard, Drop D, Eb, D standard, Drop C, DADGAD, Open G) and play a single string (ideally open, with a clean tone).
  • The tuner recognizes which string you're tuning – it shows its note (e.g. E2) and the deviation in cents with a needle.
  • Green = in tune, orange = close, red = far. Needle on the left (♭) – tune up, on the right (♯) – tune down.
  • A tuned string turns green (✓) in the row of strings, so you see which ones are done.
  • Signal at the bottom shows whether the tuner hears anything at all. “Silence” means nothing comes from the input – check the label at the top and the volume on ToneX.
  • Concert A (440 Hz) can be shifted with the − and + buttons (e.g. 442 Hz when playing with a piano).

5. Metronome and drums

Metronome

  • Tap ♩ 100 to turn it on/off.
  • Long-press to set the tempo – as a number or by tapping along (at least three taps).

Drums

  • Tap 🥁 Rock to start/stop the drums (instead of the metronome).
  • Long-press to choose the style: Rock, Metal, Reggae, Ballad, Blues shuffle, Funk, Punk, 6/8, 3/4; then the fill (every 4 or 8 bars, no fills), volume and tempo. Changes are heard immediately.
  • Drums and metronome go to the ToneX headphones. If you want them from the phone, turn on “Metronome and drums to the phone” in the settings.
  • When you record with drums, the recording stays pure guitar; the style, tempo and bar start are saved with the riff.
The drum sounds come from the Virtuosity Drums kit (Versilian Studios, free CC0 license).

6. Looper

The looper records a short loop that plays over and over, and you can record more layers on top. FretSnap takes care of the timing for you.

  1. Tap 🔁 Looper – the panel opens.
  2. Choose the loop length: 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars. The tempo is the metronome tempo (long-press ♩).
  3. Tap ● Record loop. A stick count-in plays and you see “Starts in 2… 1…”.
  4. Play. Recording stops by itself exactly at the end of the last bar and the loop starts repeating right away. You don't have to hit anything.
  5. ● Add layer records another layer – it starts at the beginning of the loop and lasts exactly one cycle.
  6. Undo removes the last layer. ⋮ saves the loop as a riff or clears it.
  • The cursor in the panel shows where you are in the loop; the marks are bars and beats. Everything turns red while recording.
  • While the loop plays you can turn on the metronome or drums – they stay in time with the loop.
  • While the looper holds a loop, the tempo can't change (the loop wouldn't fit). Clear it first.
  • If the looper stops (e.g. when you play a riff), ▶ Play loop starts it again after a count-in.

6a. Chord backing

FretSnap plays bass and keys along with the drums following a chord progression – for practicing solos and improvisation.

  1. Tap 🎹 Backing.
  2. Choose the key (Key ▾) and a ready-made progression – pop I–V–vi–IV, ballad, rock, 12-bar blues, minor progressions…
  3. You can edit the progression: tap a chord to change or remove it, + adds another. Chords that belong to the key are offered.
  4. Set whether a chord lasts 1 or 2 bars and what plays: bass, keys (piano), drums (set the drum style and tempo by long-pressing 🥁 and ♩).
  5. ▶ Play – after a count-in it plays over and over. The chord being played is highlighted.
  6. 🎼 Matching scale shows the key's scale on the fretboard; green notes belong to the chord being played – good notes to land on.

The backing goes to ToneX like the drums and fits the looper: you can record a rhythm part over it and play a solo.

The bass uses Growlybass samples (Karoryfer Samples), the piano the Versilian Community Sample Library – both under the free CC0 license.

6b. Scale on the fretboard

  • Tap 🎼 Scale and choose the root note (C to B) and the scale: minor/major pentatonic, blues, natural minor, major, Dorian, Mixolydian, harmonic minor, Phrygian.
  • The fretboard shows the notes of the scale with their names; the root is in the accent color. The fretboard follows the tuning chosen in the tuner.
  • Whole neck or Position 1, 2… – a position highlights the notes within a five-fret window.
  • For a riff, ⋮ → Scale and key: FretSnap detects the key from the recording (e.g. E minor) and sets the matching scale. If it's wrong, change it above.

7. Riff list

  • Riffs are grouped by day. Each shows its length, tempo, drum style, song, number of markers and a waveform.
  • Filters: All, ★ Favorites, 🎵 Songs. 🔍 searches names, notes, keys and songs (accents don't matter).
  • Tap a riff to play it, ☆ adds it to favorites.
  • Long-press a riff to start selecting several riffs at once – then you can favorite, send, zip, send for testing or delete them.
  • A deletion can still be undone for a moment with the Undo button at the bottom.

The ⋮ menu of a riff

Note, tempo, key
Your own description of the riff.
🎛 Tone (ToneX)
Which tone or preset you used (e.g. “Marshall crunch, preset 3”). It's shown with the riff, you can search for it, and new recordings get it automatically until you change it (after saving a riff the message has a Tone button).
🎵 Add to a song
See Songs.
🎼 Scale and key
Detects the riff's key and shows the matching scale on the fretboard.
Rename
If Android asks for permission to modify the file, confirm (this applies to riffs from a previous installation).
Detect the tempo from the playing
FretSnap finds the hits in the recording and determines the tempo and where the bar starts. For a freely played riff it says it can't – then enter the tempo by hand.
Send as WAV / M4A
WAV is full quality, M4A a smaller file (for messages, e-mail).
🥁 Send with drums
The riff mixed with drums in its tempo and style (M4A). The original stays clean.
Send for testing (Drive)
Sends the riff to the Drive app, with an optional note of what you played.

8. Player

  • A small player appears at the bottom; ⌃ expands it.
  • Waveform: tap to jump to a spot. Drag your finger to mark a part that plays over and over (a loop).
  • Speed slows playback down without changing the pitch (50–125 %), Repeat plays it again and again.
  • The Start [ / End ] / Whole buttons set the loop at the current playing position.
  • 🥁 Drums with the riff plays drums in the riff's tempo and style; they follow it even when slowed down or looped. If the riff has no tempo, FretSnap detects it first. The first time you play with drums, the beat positions are fine-tuned to how you played.
  • 📍 + adds a marker where it's playing. Markers are yellow on the waveform and shown below it as buttons: tap = jump, long-press = delete.

9. Songs

  • In a riff's ⋮ choose Add to a song, type a song (or pick an existing one) and a part – Intro, Verse, Chorus, Solo…
  • The 🎵 Songs filter groups riffs by song in their order.
  • Change the order in ⋮ → Move up / down in the song.
  • Tap a song name to play its parts one after another.
  • Remove a riff from a song in the same window with the Remove button.

10. Sharing and backup

  • Riffs are stored on the phone in the Music/FretSnap folder (other apps see them too). Sent M4A files are in Music/FretSnap/Shared. Up to version 1.12 the app was called Riffy; recordings from Music/Riffy are moved over automatically.
  • Notes, tempo, favorites, songs and markers are stored right inside the files – they survive reinstalling and copying to a computer.
  • Backup (Settings): Back up new sends riffs since the last backup to the Drive app, Back up everything sends all of them. In Drive, save them to one folder (e.g. “FretSnap backup”).

11. Settings (⚙)

Input
Where to record from. USB devices come first. “Find settings” tries variants for other audio interfaces, “USB report” prepares a technical description of the device (to send when there are problems). The 🎸 Acoustic guitar via microphone option is described in section 1a.
Recording
Trimming silence, evening out loudness, automatic recording, keeping the screen on, where the metronome and drums go.
Test tone to ToneX
Three beeps in the ToneX headphones – checks that sound from FretSnap reaches ToneX.
Appearance and language
Theme (follow the phone, dark, light), accent color and language (follow the phone, Slovak, English).
Backup
See above.
App
Version, checking for updates and this guide. FretSnap also checks for a new version by itself once a day.

12. When something doesn't work

ToneX is connected but there's silence
Wait a few seconds (ToneX wakes up after being plugged in). If that doesn't help, unplug it and plug it in again. Still nothing? ⚙ → USB report → send the report.
The sound clips (red edge on the bar)
Tap “Gain ▾” and turn it down, or lower the output on ToneX.
Metronome/drums can't be heard in the ToneX headphones
Check that “Metronome and drums to the phone” is off and try ⚙ → Test tone to ToneX.
The drums don't fit the riff
Try ⋮ → Detect the tempo again from the playing. If the drums start the bar on the wrong beat, correct the tempo by hand.
Looper layers are out of time
Let us know whether the second layer is early or late – the offset can be fine-tuned.
A riff can't be renamed or deleted
It's a riff from a previous installation; Android asks for permission – confirm it.
